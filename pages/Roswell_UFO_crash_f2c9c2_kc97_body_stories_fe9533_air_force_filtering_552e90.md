## Introduction

When the U.S. [Air Force]({{ 'air-force/' | relative_url }}) revisited Roswell body-recovery claims in its 1997 report *The Roswell Report: [Case Closed]({{ 'case-closed/' | relative_url }})*, investigators did not simply search for any fatal accident near Roswell. They developed a filtering test aimed at identifying real events that could plausibly have generated later stories about bodies, autopsies, and unusual activity at the Roswell Army Air Field hospital. The goal was to separate documented military incidents from later legend-building and to determine whether witness accounts contained details that matched actual events rather than an alleged extraterrestrial crash.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2010/Oct/27/2001330219/-1/-1/0/AFD-101027-030.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AFD 101027 030</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarU.S. Air Force: &quot;The Roswell Report: Case Closed&quot;It appears that UFO proponents have failed to establish the accurate da...</span></span></span>

The Air Force's approach was narrow and practical. Instead of asking whether an accident occurred near Roswell, researchers asked whether it involved the specific elements repeatedly described in body-recovery stories: severely damaged human remains, transport to a military hospital, and post-mortem examinations. That screening process ultimately led investigators to focus on the 1956 [KC-97 crash]({{ 'kc-97-crash/' | relative_url }}) rather than other accidents associated with Walker Air Force Base.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2010/Oct/27/2001330219/-1/-1/0/AFD-101027-030.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AFD 101027 030</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarU.S. Air Force: &quot;The Roswell Report: Case Closed&quot;It appears that UFO proponents have failed to establish the accurate da...</span></span></span>

## The Three Criteria Used to Compare Fatal Accidents

The Air Force faced a problem common to historical investigations: many accidents had occurred around Roswell, but only a few resembled the narratives later attached to the Roswell legend.

Researchers therefore examined documented incidents against three recurring features found in witness stories:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. **Were the victims badly burned or otherwise difficult to identify?**
2. **Were the remains taken to the base hospital?**
3. **Were autopsies or formal examinations performed?**

</div>

These criteria emerged because the most influential body-story accounts involved descriptions of unusual corpses, mortuary inquiries, hospital activity, body transport, and examinations after death. Investigators reasoned that if a real event inspired such memories, it should leave a documentary trail matching those characteristics.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2010/Oct/27/2001330219/-1/-1/0/AFD-101027-030.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AFD 101027 030</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarU.S. Air Force: &quot;The Roswell Report: Case Closed&quot;It appears that UFO proponents have failed to establish the accurate da...</span></span></span>

The 1956 [KC-97]({{ 'kc-97/' | relative_url }}) tanker crash met all three conditions. According to the Air Force report, the aircraft was destroyed by fire after a catastrophic propeller failure. The remains of the eleven airmen were burned, recovered by military personnel, taken to the Walker AFB hospital for identification work, and subjected to autopsy procedures. The identification effort was difficult enough that specialists were brought in and some work was moved to refrigerated facilities because of the condition of the remains.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gutenberg.org/files/63659/63659-h/63659-h.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gutenberg.org">[Project Gutenberg]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gutenberg.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Gutenberg The Roswell Report: Case Closed, by James Mc Andrew—</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">alien bodies associated with the “Roswell Incident.” Pursuant to the... duty I considered unusual or that might explain reports of bodie...</span></span></span>

From the perspective of the filtering test, that combination was significant because it reproduced many of the practical details that later appeared in Roswell body narratives without requiring any extraterrestrial explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2010/Oct/27/2001330219/-1/-1/0/AFD-101027-030.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AFD 101027 030</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarU.S. Air Force: &quot;The Roswell Report: Case Closed&quot;It appears that UFO proponents have failed to establish the accurate da...</span></span></span>

## Why Other Walker AFB Accidents Were Rejected

The Air Force did not argue that every accident near Roswell contributed to UFO stories. Rather, the filtering process excluded incidents that failed one or more of the key criteria.

Many aircraft accidents produced fatalities but lacked the distinctive chain of events described in hospital-based Roswell accounts. Some involved deaths without extensive recovery and identification procedures. Others did not generate reports of remains being transported through the base hospital system or subjected to the sort of examinations later described by witnesses. Still others occurred under circumstances that did not involve severely burned [bodies]({{ 'bodies/' | relative_url }}) or the unusual handling measures that became central to later stories.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2010/Oct/27/2001330219/-1/-1/0/AFD-101027-030.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AFD 101027 030</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarU.S. Air Force: &quot;The Roswell Report: Case Closed&quot;It appears that UFO proponents have failed to establish the accurate da...</span></span></span>

The Air Force's broader investigation also concluded that body-related Roswell claims were not connected to the 1947 [Project Mogul]({{ 'project-mogul/' | relative_url }}) debris recovery. Researchers stated that Mogul equipment could not have carried passengers and that no evidence showed the Army Air Forces conducting body-recovery operations in the Roswell area during the period associated with the original debris incident. As a result, accidents and operations from later years became the focus of comparison instead.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.af.mil/The-Roswell-Report/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: af.mil">[U.S. Air Force]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Air ForceThe Roswell Report... crash and recovery of an extraterrestrial vehicle and its alien occupants near Roswell, N.M..... ali...</span></span></span>

This process substantially narrowed the field. Rather than treating every crash as a possible source, investigators concentrated on events whose documented aftermath resembled the specific details contained in witness recollections.

## What the Filtering Method Proves and Does Not Prove

The filtering test was designed to identify plausible historical sources for later stories. It was not designed to prove exactly how any particular witness formed a memory or whether every detail in a Roswell account came from a single event.

What the method does show is that at least one documented accident near Roswell contained a combination of elements often presented as mysterious: burned bodies, transport to the military hospital, outside specialists, identification difficulties, and autopsies. Those features existed in the historical record independent of any claim about alien bodies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gutenberg.org/files/63659/63659-h/63659-h.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gutenberg.org">[Project Gutenberg]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gutenberg.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Gutenberg The Roswell Report: Case Closed, by James Mc Andrew—</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">alien bodies associated with the “Roswell Incident.” Pursuant to the... duty I considered unusual or that might explain reports of bodie...</span></span></span>

What the method does **not** demonstrate is that every witness was remembering the KC-97 crash, that all body stories originated from that accident, or that [memory contamination]({{ 'memory-drift/' | relative_url }}) can be proven in a specific individual case. The Air Force argument was fundamentally one of plausibility and comparison.
