## Introduction

The U.S. [Air Force]({{ 'air-force/' | relative_url }}) did not reopen the Roswell case in the 1990s because new physical evidence had been discovered at the crash site. Instead, Roswell became an official government [records]({{ 'records/' | relative_url }}) issue. The immediate trigger was a congressional inquiry by Representative Steven Schiff of New Mexico, who sought answers about longstanding claims that the government had concealed information about a 1947 crash near Roswell. When efforts to obtain records produced conflicting responses, the issue moved beyond UFO folklore and into the realm of congressional oversight, records management, and government accountability. The result was a General Accounting Office (GAO) investigation and a large-scale Air Force search of archives, records centres, and historical files that eventually produced the Roswell reports of 1994 and 1997.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying ObjectsSchiff (R-NM), the General Accounting Office (GAO) initiated an audit in...</span></span></span>

## Steven Schiff's Congressional Request

The key figure behind the reopening was Representative Steven H. Schiff, whose congressional district included Roswell. Schiff was not initially conducting a campaign to prove extraterrestrial visitation. Rather, he became concerned about whether federal agencies were properly responding to requests for information about the incident. According to contemporary accounts, he sought a Department of Defense briefing and information concerning the 1947 event after hearing from constituents and researchers interested in the case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1994/01/14/gao-turns-to-alien-turf-in-probe/a42ab4bd-cd82-4c1c-a88a-3800e10616c4/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: washingtonpost.com">[The Washington Post]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">washingtonpost.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Washington PostGAO TURNS TO ALIEN TURF IN PROBE13 Jan 1994 — Schiff said after calling Aspin last March to request a Defense Departme...</span></span></span>

What transformed the matter into a formal government inquiry was Schiff's frustration with the answers he received. He was reportedly told that relevant records had been transferred to the National Archives, yet archive officials indicated they did not possess the expected Roswell files. This apparent gap raised a broader question: if records should exist, where were they?<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1994/01/14/gao-turns-to-alien-turf-in-probe/a42ab4bd-cd82-4c1c-a88a-3800e10616c4/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: washingtonpost.com">[The Washington Post]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">washingtonpost.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Washington PostGAO TURNS TO ALIEN TURF IN PROBE13 Jan 1994 — Schiff said after calling Aspin last March to request a Defense Departme...</span></span></span>

For Schiff, the issue increasingly became one of accountability rather than belief in UFO claims. He argued that government responsiveness and record handling were matters worthy of investigation regardless of what had actually crashed in 1947. That concern helped bring the GAO into the picture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1994/01/14/gao-turns-to-alien-turf-in-probe/a42ab4bd-cd82-4c1c-a88a-3800e10616c4/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: washingtonpost.com">[The Washington Post]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">washingtonpost.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Washington PostGAO TURNS TO ALIEN TURF IN PROBE13 Jan 1994 — Schiff said after calling Aspin last March to request a Defense Departme...</span></span></span>

## How the GAO Turned Roswell into a Records Investigation

In early 1994, the GAO—Congress's investigative and auditing arm—opened a formal review. The purpose was not to determine whether aliens had visited Earth. Instead, the GAO was asked to identify government records concerning the Roswell incident and determine whether those records had been properly managed under applicable regulations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sgp.fas.org/othergov/roswell.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sgp.fas.org">[FAS Project on Government Secrecy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sgp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FAS Project on Government SecrecyGAO Report on Roswell, NM UFO CrashIn the July 1994 Report of Air Force Research Regarding the Roswell I...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1994</span></span></span>

The GAO's inquiry examined how air accidents and similar incidents were supposed to be documented and preserved. Investigators searched records across multiple agencies, including the Department of Defense, the FBI, and other federal repositories. They reviewed both classified and unclassified material from the period surrounding the 1947 event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sgp.fas.org/othergov/roswell.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sgp.fas.org">[FAS Project on Government Secrecy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sgp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FAS Project on Government SecrecyGAO Report on Roswell, NM UFO CrashIn the July 1994 Report of Air Force Research Regarding the Roswell I...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1994</span></span></span>

This distinction is important. Roswell was not reopened as a fresh field investigation involving excavation, forensic recovery, or witness re-interviews alone. It was reopened because Congress wanted to know whether relevant government records existed, whether they had been destroyed, and whether agencies had adequately responded to inquiries about them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying ObjectsSchiff (R-NM), the General Accounting Office (GAO) initiated an audit in...</span></span></span>

## What the Air Force Was Asked to Answer

Because the Roswell incident involved military personnel and Army Air Forces facilities in 1947, the Air Force became the central agency in the search. During discussions between the GAO and government departments, it became clear that most of the investigation would focus on Air Force records systems and historical archives. The Air Force itself later acknowledged that the audit's practical purpose was to locate information concerning the Roswell incident.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/ufo/report_af_roswell.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nsa.gov">[NSA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nsa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">report of air force research regarding theMarch 24, 2018 — 21 Jul 1994 — This memorandum indicated that the &quot;GAO is anxious to respond...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 24, 2018</span></span></span>

The Air Force was effectively asked three broad questions:

* Did the government possess records concerning the Roswell recovery?
* Had any relevant records been improperly destroyed, lost, or mishandled?
* Did available documentation support claims that an extraterrestrial craft or alien [bodies]({{ 'bodies/' | relative_url }}) had been recovered?<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://search.worldcat.org/title/The-Roswell-report-%3A-case-closed/oclc/645867494" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: search.worldcat.org">[WorldCat]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">search.worldcat.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Roswell report: case closed... Officials also interviewed individuals who might have had knowledge of the events. ## Why the Search Expanded Beyond a Simple File Hunt

The Air Force's records search produced an unexpected result. While investigators were attempting to answer a records-management question, they uncovered evidence relating to [Project Mogul]({{ 'project-mogul/' | relative_url }}), a previously classified balloon programme designed to detect Soviet nuclear activity. Researchers concluded that the recovered [debris]({{ 'debris/' | relative_url }}) was most consistent with material from one of these balloon arrays.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/AFD-101201-038.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dafhistory.af.mil">[DAF History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dafhistory.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on af.mil.</span></span></span>

This discovery changed the character of the investigation. What began as a search for [missing records]({{ 'missing-records/' | relative_url }}) evolved into an effort to explain why the original 1947 "weather balloon" story had differed from the actual classified programme involved. The Air Force therefore moved beyond simply reporting on archives and produced a historical reconstruction of the event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/AFD-101201-038.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dafhistory.af.mil">[DAF History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dafhistory.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on af.mil.</span></span></span>

The resulting 1994 report, *The Roswell Report: Fact vs. Fiction in the New Mexico Desert*, was intended to answer the questions raised by the GAO inquiry and congressional oversight process. Later, continuing public interest in reports of alien bodies led to a second report, *The Roswell Report: Case Closed*, in 1997.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying ObjectsSchiff (R-NM), the General Accounting Office (GAO) initiated an audit in...</span></span></span>

## Why the Reopening Matters

The significance of the 1990s reopening lies in the reason it happened. Roswell did not return to official attention because the government suddenly reconsidered the extraterrestrial hypothesis. It returned because a member of Congress questioned the handling and availability of records connected to a famous historical controversy. The Air Force response was therefore shaped by governance, archival accountability, and public transparency concerns as much as by the underlying UFO claims.
