## Introduction

Among all the attempts to connect [Project Mogul]({{ 'project-mogul/' | relative_url }}) to the Roswell debris discovery, one event has occupied the centre of the debate: Flight 4, a [balloon train]({{ 'balloon-train/' | relative_url }}) launched from Alamogordo Army Air Field on 4 June 1947. The significance of Flight 4 is not that it conclusively solves the Roswell mystery, but that it provides the most specific and documented pathway between a known military project and debris reportedly found on the Foster ranch several weeks later. For supporters of the Mogul explanation, Flight 4 is the strongest candidate because it was launched in the right period, travelled toward the Corona area, and was not recovered by its operators. For critics, uncertainties in the surviving records raise questions about whether Flight 4 actually flew in the form later reconstructed. The modern Roswell debate therefore often turns less on balloons in general than on the details of this single June 1947 launch.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/FOID/Reading%20Room/UFOsandUAPs/rosswe1.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">It is very probable that this Top Secret project balloon train</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Report of Air Force Research Regarding the &quot;Roswell...September 26, 2012 — 27 Jul 1994 — Flight 4 was launched on June 4, 1947, but w...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 26, 2012</span></span></span>

## Why Flight 4 Became the Central Candidate

The focus on Flight 4 emerged largely from [Air Force]({{ 'air-force/' | relative_url }}) and Project Mogul research conducted decades after the original incident. During investigations in the 1990s, researchers examined surviving project [records]({{ 'records/' | relative_url }}), witness statements and technical documents from the New York University balloon team that operated the Mogul programme. Their conclusion was that Flight 4 represented the most likely source of the debris recovered near Roswell.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/FOID/Reading%20Room/UFOsandUAPs/rosswe1.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[whs.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">It is very probable that this Top Secret project balloon train</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Report of Air Force Research Regarding the &quot;Roswell...September 26, 2012 — 27 Jul 1994 — Flight 4 was launched on June 4, 1947, but w...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 26, 2012</span></span></span>

What made Flight 4 stand out was a combination of factors:

* It was launched on 4 June 1947, roughly a month before the Roswell publicity erupted.
* Tracking data suggested movement toward the Corona region, close to where rancher W.W. “Mac” Brazel later reported finding debris.
* The flight was not recovered by the NYU team.
* The balloon train included radar-reflector components and other materials that sceptical investigators argued resembled descriptions of the recovered wreckage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/2017/12/the-roswell-incident-at-70-facts-not-myths/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[skepticalinquirer.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Skeptical InquirerThe Roswell Incident at 70: Facts, Not Myths4. Earlier explanations had simply referred to a weather balloon. Flight 4 gave the Mogul theory a specific launch, date and trajectory, allowing investigators to connect documented military activity with a documented debris discovery rather than relying on a generic possibility.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/AFD-101201-038.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dafhistory.af.mil">[DAF History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dafhistory.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DAF HistoryThe Roswell ReportThe importance of attachment 32, Lieutenant McAndrew&#x27;s synopsis, derives from his description of Project MOG...</span></span></span>

## What the Reconstructed Launch Record Says

A major reason Flight 4 remains controversial is that its importance depends on reconstruction. Early Mogul operations were experimental, and record-keeping was not always complete. Some launches were categorised differently from others, and later investigators had to piece together events from surviving logs, diaries and [testimony]({{ 'testimony/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/AFD-101201-038.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dafhistory.af.mil">[DAF History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dafhistory.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DAF HistoryThe Roswell ReportThe importance of attachment 32, Lieutenant McAndrew&#x27;s synopsis, derives from his description of Project MOG...</span></span></span>

The reconstruction adopted by the Air Force held that Flight 4 launched on 4 June 1947 and travelled northeast from Alamogordo. Tracking was eventually lost before recovery. Investigators later argued that the balloon train could have descended in the general region where Brazel found debris. According to the 1994 Air Force report, Flight 4 was launched but never recovered by the NYU group, making it a plausible source for material later discovered on the ranch.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/FOID/Reading%20Room/UFOsandUAPs/rosswe1.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">It is very probable that this Top Secret project balloon train</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Report of Air Force Research Regarding the &quot;Roswell...September 26, 2012 — 27 Jul 1994 — Flight 4 was launched on June 4, 1947, but w...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 26, 2012</span></span></span>

Charles Moore's later testimony was important because he explained that early Mogul operations included launches and service flights that were not always reflected in the simplified records later available to outsiders. His recollections helped investigators argue that apparent gaps in documentation did not necessarily mean no flight occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/AFD-101201-038.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dafhistory.af.mil">[DAF History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dafhistory.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DAF HistoryThe Roswell ReportThe importance of attachment 32, Lieutenant McAndrew&#x27;s synopsis, derives from his description of Project MOG...</span></span></span>

At the same time, critics have pointed to surviving notes from project scientist Albert Crary that appear to create ambiguity about the flight. Some researchers have argued that these notes suggest a planned launch configuration was altered or cancelled, raising questions about whether the later Flight 4 reconstruction accurately reflects what happened on 4 June. Those disputes became one of the principal challenges to the Mogul explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362854511_A_Grounded_Theory_Update_on_the_Roswell_UFO_Incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A Grounded Theory Update on the Roswell UFO IncidentProject Mogul does not provide the ultimate answer. ## Why Recovery Uncertainty Matters

Flight 4's status as the leading Mogul candidate depends heavily on the fact that it was not recovered by the project team. If a balloon train had been launched, tracked and retrieved, it would be difficult to connect it to debris found elsewhere weeks later. The missing recovery record is therefore essential to the argument.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/FOID/Reading%20Room/UFOsandUAPs/rosswe1.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">It is very probable that this Top Secret project balloon train</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Report of Air Force Research Regarding the &quot;Roswell...September 26, 2012 — 27 Jul 1994 — Flight 4 was launched on June 4, 1947, but w...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 26, 2012</span></span></span>

Supporters of the Mogul explanation view the absence of recovery as exactly what would be expected if the train descended in remote ranchland. New Mexico's terrain covered enormous areas with limited roads, sparse population and few reliable means of communication. A balloon train drifting beyond tracking range could disappear without immediate retrieval.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/FOID/Reading%20Room/UFOsandUAPs/rosswe1.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">It is very probable that this Top Secret project balloon train</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Report of Air Force Research Regarding the &quot;Roswell...September 26, 2012 — 27 Jul 1994 — Flight 4 was launched on June 4, 1947, but w...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 26, 2012</span></span></span>

Critics respond that uncertainty cuts both ways. A flight that cannot be fully documented may be difficult to rule out, but it is also difficult to prove. ## How Flight 4 Fits the Roswell Debris Timeline

The chronology is one of the strongest arguments in Flight 4's favour. The launch occurred on 4 June 1947.
