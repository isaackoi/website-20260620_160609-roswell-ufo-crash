## Introduction

The reason 4 June 1947 became the key date in the Roswell debate is straightforward: among the known [Project Mogul]({{ 'project-mogul/' | relative_url }}) launches, the early June Flight 4 is the one that most closely matches the reported timing of rancher W. W. “Mac” Brazel’s discovery. If the debris was found in mid-June, as Brazel originally indicated, then balloon flights launched in July become difficult candidates. That timing issue led Air Force investigators in the 1990s to focus on Mogul Flight 4, a [balloon train]({{ 'balloon-train/' | relative_url }}) launched from Alamogordo on 4 June and apparently not recovered by its operators.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muller.lbl.gov/teaching/physics10/Roswell/USMogulReport.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muller.lbl.gov">[Muller Lab]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muller.lbl.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Muller LabProject MogulSince the launch was unsuccessful, no flight number was assigned. the debris found by the rancher. ## Brazel's June Discovery Window

The strongest reason Flight 4 emerged as the Roswell candidate was the reported date of discovery.

In statements attributed to Brazel in July 1947, he indicated that he found the unusual debris weeks before the story became public. Air Force researchers later interpreted those statements as placing the discovery around 14 June. If that date is approximately correct, then debris from balloon launches conducted in July could not have been responsible because the material was already on the ground.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muller.lbl.gov/teaching/physics10/Roswell/USMogulReport.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muller.lbl.gov">[Muller Lab]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muller.lbl.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Muller LabProject MogulSince the launch was unsuccessful, no flight number was assigned. the debris found by the rancher. This timing narrowed the field dramatically. Investigators reviewing Project Mogul records concluded that only a small number of June launches remained plausible. Among those, Flight 4 attracted attention because it was not recovered and because its expected path carried it into the general region east of the Sacramento Mountains where debris was reportedly found.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muller.lbl.gov/teaching/physics10/Roswell/USMogulReport.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muller.lbl.gov">[Muller Lab]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muller.lbl.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Muller LabProject MogulSince the launch was unsuccessful, no flight number was assigned. the debris found by the rancher. The significance of the June discovery window is often overlooked. The debate is not simply about whether Mogul balloons existed near Roswell. The key question is whether any known Mogul flight could have deposited debris before Brazel encountered it. Flight 4 became central because it was one of the few launches that could satisfy that requirement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muller.lbl.gov/teaching/physics10/Roswell/USMogulReport.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muller.lbl.gov">[Muller Lab]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muller.lbl.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Muller LabProject MogulSince the launch was unsuccessful, no flight number was assigned. the debris found by the rancher. ## What Was Happening at Alamogordo on 4 June?

Project Mogul was still in its experimental phase during early June 1947. Teams at Alamogordo Army Air Field were testing long balloon trains carrying radar reflectors and experimental equipment intended to detect distant acoustic signals from possible Soviet nuclear tests. These were much more elaborate assemblies than ordinary weather balloons.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/AFD-101201-038.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dafhistory.af.mil">[DAF History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dafhistory.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AFD 101201 038</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DAF HistoryThe Roswell ReportProject MOGUL, the top-priority classified project of balloon-borne experiments, UFO wave of 1947 which bega...</span></span></span>

According to later reconstructions, Flight 4 launched on 4 June and was tracked moving east-northeast from Alamogordo. Researchers subsequently lost track of it, and no confirmed recovery was recorded. Some analyses placed the tracked route near the Corona area and relatively close to the region later associated with the Roswell debris field.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/2017/12/the-roswell-incident-at-70-facts-not-myths/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[skepticalinquirer.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Roswell Incident at 70: Facts, Not Myths</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Flight No. 4. This mattered because investigators were not looking merely for a launch that occurred before Brazel's discovery. They were looking for a launch that disappeared and therefore could have left debris unattended on [remote ranch]({{ 'remote-ranch/' | relative_url }}) land. Flight 4 fit that description better than most documented flights, which were either recovered or accounted for.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/FOID/Reading%20Room/UFOsandUAPs/rosswe1.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">It is very probable that this Top Secret project balloon train</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDReport of Air Force Research Regarding the &quot;Roswell...27 Jul 1994 — Flight 4 was launched on June 4, 1947, but was not recovered...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 4, 1947</span></span></span>

Another factor was material similarity. Air Force investigators argued that descriptions given by Brazel, intelligence officer Jesse Marcel, and others resembled components used in Mogul service flights, including balloon fragments, foil-covered radar reflector material, lightweight structural sticks, paper-like components, and instrument hardware. These similarities strengthened the argument that the debris came from a Mogul balloon train rather than a conventional aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muller.lbl.gov/teaching/physics10/Roswell/USMogulReport.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muller.lbl.gov">[Muller Lab]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muller.lbl.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Muller LabProject MogulSince the launch was unsuccessful, no flight number was assigned. the debris found by the rancher. ## Why Later Flights Fit Less Well

Once the discovery date is placed in June, later Mogul launches become increasingly difficult to reconcile with the chronology.

Most July flights occurred after the debris was supposedly already known to Brazel. Even if some records were incomplete, a July launch cannot easily explain material reportedly found weeks earlier.
