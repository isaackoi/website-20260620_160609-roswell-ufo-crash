## Introduction

One of the most revealing features of Roswell’s UFO tourism culture is not a museum display or a historical document. It is the simple act of posing for a photograph with an alien. At the Roswell Visitor Center, visitors are encouraged to take free souvenir pictures with life-sized alien figures, turning the first stop in town into a participatory attraction rather than a purely informational one. The experience demonstrates how Roswell has transformed a disputed historical story into a shared cultural performance: visitors do not merely learn about the [Roswell UFO crash]({{ 'roswell-ufo-crash/' | relative_url }}) narrative, they briefly step inside it. ## Why Everyone Takes Alien Photos in Roswell

The Roswell Visitor Center functions as both a [tourism]({{ 'tourism/' | relative_url }}) information hub and an attraction in its own right. Official tourism materials repeatedly highlight the opportunity to take a free souvenir photograph with the centre’s resident aliens before leaving. The alien figures are not hidden in a corner; they are presented as part of the visitor experience from the moment travellers arrive in town.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://seeroswell.com/museums-attractions/roswell-visitor-center/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: seeroswell.com">[Seeroswell]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">seeroswell.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell Visitor CenterBefore leaving, make sure to take a FREE souvenir photo with the Visitors Center aliens. 426 N. This design choice matters because photography has become one of the primary ways tourists document and share travel experiences. Rather than asking visitors simply to read brochures or collect maps, the Visitor Center offers a ready-made image that instantly signals “I was in Roswell.” Reviews and travel accounts consistently mention these photo opportunities as part of the attraction, with visitors noting both outdoor and indoor alien-themed picture spots.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g47182-d1759310-Reviews-Roswell_Visitors_Center-Roswell_New_Mexico.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tripadvisor.com">[tripadvisor.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tripadvisor.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Roswell Visitors Center</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">There are photo ops outside the building. ## How Staged Alien Scenes Turn Visitors into Participants

The Roswell Visitor Center's alien figures are examples of what tourism researchers often describe as staged experiences: carefully designed environments that encourage visitors to perform a role. In this case, the role is playful rather than educational. The visitor is invited to stand beside extraterrestrials, smile for the camera, and temporarily inhabit Roswell’s UFO mythology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.roadsideamerica.com/story/73478" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: roadsideamerica.com">[RoadsideAmerica.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">roadsideamerica.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell Visitor Center: Pose With AliensTitled, simply, &quot;Believe,&quot; the photo-op offers visitors the chance to pose between two friendly s...</span></span></span>

The staging is often seasonal. Reports describe the alien figures and backgrounds being updated for holidays, festivals, and local events. Rather than preserving a fixed historical display, the scene changes to remain visually fresh and relevant to repeat visitors and annual celebrations such as the UFO Festival.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.roadsideamerica.com/story/73478" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: roadsideamerica.com">[RoadsideAmerica.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">roadsideamerica.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell Visitor Center: Pose With AliensTitled, simply, &quot;Believe,&quot; the photo-op offers visitors the chance to pose between two friendly s...</span></span></span>

This approach creates what might be called a manufactured memory. The photograph does not document evidence related to the 1947 incident. Instead, it [records]({{ 'records/' | relative_url }}) participation in Roswell’s modern identity. Years later, a visitor may remember less about specific museum panels than about the image of standing between two aliens and sharing the photograph with friends.

The result is a shift from historical consumption to experiential consumption. The visitor takes away not only information but also proof of involvement.

## How Photo Ops Differ from Museum Exhibits

The contrast between the Visitor Center photo scene and Roswell’s museums helps explain why both exist side by side.

Museum exhibits generally ask visitors to evaluate information. At the International [UFO Museum]({{ 'ufo-museum/' | relative_url }}) and Research Center, guests encounter witness accounts, documents, timelines, competing interpretations, and displays related to the Roswell incident. Whether visitors leave convinced or sceptical, the museum is structured around interpretation and inquiry.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.roswellufomuseum.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: roswellufomuseum.com">[roswellufomuseum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">roswellufomuseum.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International UFO Museum and Research Center &#124; Gift Shop...Come and explore our exhibits, witness firsthand accounts, an...</span></span></span>

Photo opportunities operate differently. They require no prior knowledge, no position on the UFO debate, and no engagement with evidence. A visitor can participate regardless of whether they believe extraterrestrials ever crashed near Roswell.

The distinction can be summarised simply:
