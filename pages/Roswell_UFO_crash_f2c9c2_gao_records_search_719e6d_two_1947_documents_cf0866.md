## Introduction

When the U.S. Government Accountability Office (GAO) searched for official [records]({{ 'records/' | relative_url }}) relating to the Roswell incident, the result was unexpectedly narrow. After examining military, intelligence and archival holdings, the GAO reported that only two surviving government records created in 1947 directly referred to the event: a July 1947 history report produced by the combined 509th Bomb Group and Roswell Army Air Field (RAAF), and an FBI teletype dated 8 July 1947. These documents became disproportionately important because many other Roswell-era records had been destroyed or could not be located. As a result, debates about what happened near Roswell often return to the wording of these two contemporary records and how they compare with later witness [testimony]({{ 'testimony/' | relative_url }}) and official explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/nsiad" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[gao.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947...GAO provided information on the 1947 weather balloon crash at Roswell Army Air...</span></span></span>

## The 509th Bomb Group history report

The first document identified by the GAO was not a dramatic intelligence file or accident investigation. It was a routine monthly history report prepared by the combined 509th Bomb Group and Roswell Army Air Field. The 509th was not an ordinary unit; it was the Army [Air Forces]({{ 'air-force/' | relative_url }}) group that had carried out the atomic bomb missions during the Second World War and remained one of the most prominent military organisations in the United States. That status has often led researchers to ask whether an unusual recovery operation would have generated detailed paperwork.

The surviving history report contains a brief reference to the recovery of a “[flying disc]({{ 'flying-disc/' | relative_url }})” and notes that military authorities later determined it to be a radar-tracking balloon. The significance of the report lies less in its detail than in its timing. It was written within weeks of the event rather than decades later, making it one of the few surviving records produced by personnel close to the original chain of command.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sgp.fas.org/othergov/roswell.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sgp.fas.org">[FAS Project on Government Secrecy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sgp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FAS Project on Government SecrecyGAO Report on Roswell, NM UFO CrashOn July 8, 1947, the Roswell Army Air Field (RAAF) public information...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

For supporters of the conventional explanation, the report is important because it records the incident as having been resolved into a balloon-related identification. For critics of the official account, the document is notable for what it does not contain. It offers only a short summary and provides none of the detailed descriptions that later became central to Roswell claims. The report therefore functions more as evidence of how the military officially characterised the event in July 1947 than as a comprehensive narrative of what was recovered. ### Why the history report carries weight

Several factors explain why historians and investigators repeatedly cite this document:

* It was created contemporaneously rather than reconstructed from memory decades later.
* It originated within the military organisation most closely associated with the recovery.
* It demonstrates that the incident was important enough to appear in an official unit history.
* It reflects the military's documented understanding of the event at the time the report was written.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sgp.fas.org/othergov/roswell.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sgp.fas.org">[FAS Project on Government Secrecy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sgp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FAS Project on Government SecrecyGAO Report on Roswell, NM UFO CrashOn July 8, 1947, the Roswell Army Air Field (RAAF) public information...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

At the same time, the report's brevity limits the conclusions that can be drawn from it. It neither proves nor disproves [later claims]({{ 'later-claims/' | relative_url }}); it simply establishes what was officially recorded in a surviving 1947 military document.

## The FBI teletype from July 8

The second surviving record is arguably the most quoted document in the entire Roswell archive: an FBI teletype sent from the Dallas Field Office on 8 July 1947. The message relayed information received from military sources concerning an object recovered near Roswell. Unlike the later Air Force reports of the 1990s, this document was created on the very day that public confusion about the incident was unfolding.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vault.fbi.gov/Roswell%20UFO" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vault.fbi.gov">[fbi.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vault.fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Roswell UFOOn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell UFOOn July 8, 1947, the FBI Dallas Field Office sent a teletype regarding a “flying disc” that resembled a high altitude weath...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

The teletype describes an object that was being represented as a flying disc but then records a more specific description. According to the message, the object was hexagonal in shape, suspended beneath a balloon by a cable, and associated with what appeared to be a high-altitude weather balloon and radar reflector. The document also notes that the recovered material was being transported for examination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.jasoncolavito.com/the-roswell-teletype.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jasoncolavito.com">[jasoncolavito.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jasoncolavito.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">JASON COLAVITOThe Roswell TeletypeTHE DISC IS HEXAGONAL IN SHAPE AND WAS SUSPENDED FROM A BALLON BY CABLE, WHICH BALLON WAS APPROXIMATELY...</span></span></span>

This wording has become central to Roswell research because it is one of the earliest surviving written descriptions of the recovered object. Later investigators pointed out that radar reflectors attached to balloons were indeed used in military and meteorological operations. Decades afterward, Air Force investigations linked the Roswell debris to the highly classified [Project Mogul]({{ 'project-mogul/' | relative_url }}) balloon programme, which employed [balloon trains]({{ 'balloon-train/' | relative_url }}) and radar targets intended to detect Soviet nuclear tests.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.jasoncolavito.com/the-roswell-teletype.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jasoncolavito.com">[jasoncolavito.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jasoncolavito.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">JASON COLAVITOThe Roswell TeletypeTHE DISC IS HEXAGONAL IN SHAPE AND WAS SUSPENDED FROM A BALLON BY CABLE, WHICH BALLON WAS APPROXIMATELY...</span></span></span>

### Why the teletype remains controversial

The FBI message is often cited by both sceptics and believers because it contains language that can be interpreted in different ways.

Supporters of a balloon explanation emphasise that the document itself records a balloon-and-radar-reflector interpretation only hours after the incident entered public view. The description of a suspended object and attached balloon is broadly consistent with balloon-borne equipment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.jasoncolavito.com/the-roswell-teletype.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jasoncolavito.com">[jasoncolavito.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jasoncolavito.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">JASON COLAVITOThe Roswell TeletypeTHE DISC IS HEXAGONAL IN SHAPE AND WAS SUSPENDED FROM A BALLON BY CABLE, WHICH BALLON WAS APPROXIMATELY...</span></span></span>

Those who question the official explanation point to another sentence in the teletype stating that conversations with personnel at Wright Field had not fully confirmed the initial identification. They argue that the message reflects uncertainty rather than a settled conclusion. Because the document was transmitted during a rapidly evolving situation, they view it as evidence that military authorities were still trying to determine what had been recovered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.jasoncolavito.com/the-roswell-teletype.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jasoncolavito.com">[JASON COLAVITO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jasoncolavito.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">JASON COLAVITOThe Roswell TeletypeTHE DISC IS HEXAGONAL IN SHAPE AND WAS SUSPENDED FROM A BALLON BY CABLE, WHICH BALLON WAS APPROXIMATELY...</span></span></span>

The document therefore serves as evidence of both an early explanation and an early uncertainty, which is why it continues to occupy a central place in Roswell debates.
