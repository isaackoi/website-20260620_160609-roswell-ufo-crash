## Introduction

[Glenn Dennis]({{ 'glenn-dennis/' | relative_url }}) became one of the most influential witnesses in the evolution of the Roswell UFO story because he supplied something earlier versions largely lacked: a direct link between the Roswell base hospital, alleged non-human bodies, and mortuary procedures. His account helped transform Roswell from a story about unusual wreckage into a story about alien corpses and autopsies. At the same time, Dennis's testimony became one of the most disputed elements of the entire case. Critics argue that parts of his narrative closely resemble events surrounding a real 1956 military aircraft disaster at Walker Air Force Base, while supporters maintain that he was describing genuine 1947 experiences. The resulting debate is often called the "mortician problem" because Dennis's account sits at the centre of the strongest [body claims]({{ 'body-claims/' | relative_url }}) and some of the strongest credibility challenges.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/FOID/Reading%20Room/UFOsandUAPs/RoswellReportCaseClosed.pdf?ver=2017-05-22-113519-430" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Roswell Report Case Closed</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDThe Roswell report22 May 2017 — Glenn Dennis, who claimed he was a 22-year-old mortician at the Ballard Funeral Home in Roswell in...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2017</span></span></span>

## Did Glenn Dennis Remember the Wrong Disaster?

Dennis worked for Ballard Funeral Home in Roswell and had dealings with the local military installation. Beginning in the late 1980s and early 1990s, he told researchers that in July 1947 he received unusual telephone calls from personnel at the Roswell Army Air Field asking about small caskets and about preserving [bodies]({{ 'bodies/' | relative_url }}) that had been exposed to the elements for several days. He also claimed that he encountered unusual activity at the base hospital and later spoke with a military nurse who allegedly described the autopsy of strange small bodies. According to Dennis, the nurse was deeply disturbed by what she had seen and later disappeared from the area.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0%2C33009%2C986565-3%2C00.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: content.time.com">[time.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">content.time.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DID ALIENS REALLY LAND?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DID ALIENS REALLY LAND? - TIME23 Jun 1997 — Glenn Dennis, who in 1947 was 22 and working as a mortician. These claims became enormously important because they appeared to provide a practical chain of evidence. Rather than relying on rumours about distant crash sites, Dennis's story connected alleged bodies to hospital procedures, transportation, storage, and autopsy work. Roswell books in the early 1990s elevated him into one of the case's central witnesses, and his account was repeated in documentaries, television programmes, and UFO literature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Dennis" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Glenn Dennis, a key witness in the alleged 1947 crash of a...</span></span></span>

The difficulty is that many of the details emerged more than four decades after the events Dennis described. Researchers on all sides have noted the challenge of evaluating memories recovered after such a long interval, particularly when the witness had become aware of the growing Roswell controversy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0%2C33009%2C986565-3%2C00.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: content.time.com">[Time]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">content.time.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DID ALIENS REALLY LAND?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DID ALIENS REALLY LAND? - TIME23 Jun 1997 — Glenn Dennis, who in 1947 was 22 and working as a mortician. ## What Dennis Said About Calls, Caskets, and Autopsies

Dennis's account contained several distinct elements that are sometimes discussed together even though they raise different evidential questions.

**The telephone calls.** Dennis said military personnel contacted the funeral home seeking information about unusually small caskets and asking technical questions about preserving remains. Those calls became one of the most frequently cited pieces of Roswell body testimony because they seemed difficult to explain if [no bodies]({{ 'no-bodies/' | relative_url }}) were involved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0%2C33009%2C986565-3%2C00.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: content.time.com">[Time]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">content.time.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DID ALIENS REALLY LAND?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DID ALIENS REALLY LAND? - TIME23 Jun 1997 — Glenn Dennis, who in 1947 was 22 and working as a mortician. Dennis told of receiving the...</span></span></span>

**The hospital encounter.** Dennis claimed he visited the base hospital and found unusual activity, including personnel attempting to keep people away from certain areas. He described seeing a nurse who appeared distressed and physically ill.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.popsci.com/science/article/2013-07/your-burning-roswell-ufo-questions-answered/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: popsci.com">[Popular Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">popsci.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Popular ScienceYour Burning Questions About The Roswell Incident...8 Jul 2013 — He saw Naomi Maria Selff, a nurse, run from a room with...</span></span></span>

**The nurse's story.** According to Dennis, the nurse later told him she had assisted with examinations of small, non-human-looking bodies. This alleged conversation became one of the foundations of later Roswell autopsy narratives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.popsci.com/science/article/2013-07/your-burning-roswell-ufo-questions-answered/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: popsci.com">[Popular Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">popsci.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Popular ScienceYour Burning Questions About The Roswell Incident...8 Jul 2013 — He saw Naomi Maria Selff, a nurse, run from a room with...</span></span></span>

Each part of the story depends heavily on Dennis's memory because there is little independent documentation supporting the claims. The nurse element proved especially important because it was presented as corroboration from an alleged medical insider rather than from Dennis himself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/FOID/Reading%20Room/UFOsandUAPs/RoswellReportCaseClosed.pdf?ver=2017-05-22-113519-430" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Roswell Report Case Closed</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDThe Roswell report22 May 2017 — Glenn Dennis, who claimed he was a 22-year-old mortician at the Ballard Funeral Home in Roswell in...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2017</span></span></span>

## Why the KC-97 Crash Matched Parts of His Account

The strongest sceptical challenge emerged from the U.S. Air Force's later investigation. In *The Roswell Report: Case Closed*, investigators argued that Dennis may have combined memories from different periods, especially the aftermath of a catastrophic 1956 KC-97 tanker crash near Walker Air Force Base.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/FOID/Reading%20Room/UFOsandUAPs/RoswellReportCaseClosed.pdf?ver=2017-05-22-113519-430" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Roswell Report Case Closed</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDThe Roswell report22 May 2017 — Glenn Dennis, who claimed he was a 22-year-old mortician at the Ballard Funeral Home in Roswell in...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2017</span></span></span>

That accident killed all eleven crew members. The Air Force documented that severely burned remains were brought to the base hospital, that identification work became difficult, that strong odours were a problem, and that autopsy-related procedures involved both military and civilian personnel. Some work reportedly had to be moved because of conditions associated with the remains. These are precisely the kinds of details that later appeared in Dennis's Roswell narrative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/FOID/Reading%20Room/UFOsandUAPs/RoswellReportCaseClosed.pdf?ver=2017-05-22-113519-430" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Roswell Report Case Closed</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDThe Roswell report22 May 2017 — Glenn Dennis, who claimed he was a 22-year-old mortician at the Ballard Funeral Home in Roswell in...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2017</span></span></span>

## Why the Nurse Became the Biggest Credibility Problem

The most damaging challenge to Dennis's account concerned the identity of the nurse.

When investigators attempted to verify the existence of the nurse he described, [records]({{ 'records/' | relative_url }}) searches failed to locate the individual under the names Dennis provided. Over time, he offered different names, including Naomi Self, Naomi Maria Selff, and Naomi Sipes. None could be confirmed as matching a Roswell military nurse connected to the events he described. Eventually Dennis acknowledged that at least some of the names were not real.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roswell_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Roswell incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell incident</span></span></span>

This became a major problem because the nurse was the principal source for the alleged autopsy information. If her identity could not be established, the most dramatic portion of the story lost its independent witness. Critics argued that the inability to verify the nurse seriously weakened the entire account. Some researchers who were otherwise sympathetic to Roswell claims concluded that Dennis's testimony could no longer be treated as reliable in its original form.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roswell_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Roswell incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell incident</span></span></span>

## Where the Time-Shift Explanation Remains Disputed

The KC-97 explanation has not settled the matter because it does not perfectly account for every element of Dennis's story.

Supporters of Dennis point out that the Air Force explanation is itself a reconstruction. It proposes a memory-compression process but cannot directly prove that Dennis confused the 1956 accident with alleged 1947 events. They also note that Dennis claimed to have spoken about some aspects of the story long before Roswell became a global cultural phenomenon, although the evidence for exactly what he said and when remains debated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Dennis" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Glenn Dennis, a key witness in the alleged 1947 crash of a...</span></span></span>
