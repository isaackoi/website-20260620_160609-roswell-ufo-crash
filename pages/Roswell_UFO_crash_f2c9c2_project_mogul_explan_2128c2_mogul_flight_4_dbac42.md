## Introduction

The strongest single link between [Project Mogul]({{ 'project-mogul/' | relative_url }}) and the Roswell debris is not the programme as a whole but one specific launch: New York University (NYU) Flight 4, launched from Alamogordo Army Air Field on 4 June 1947. The importance of Flight 4 is straightforward. If a [Mogul balloon train]({{ 'balloon-train/' | relative_url }}) disappeared in the right area and was never recovered, it could provide a concrete source for the debris found weeks later on ranch land near Corona, north-west of Roswell. If Flight 4 did not fly, or if its path could not plausibly have reached the debris field, then a major pillar of the official explanation becomes weaker. As a result, the debate over Roswell often narrows to a surprisingly specific question: what really happened to Mogul Flight 4?<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/AFD-101201-038.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dafhistory.af.mil">[DAF History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dafhistory.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DAF HistoryThe Roswell Report... ## The June 4 Launch from Alamogordo

According to the [Air Force]({{ 'air-force/' | relative_url }})'s Roswell investigations and later accounts from Project [Mogul]({{ 'mogul/' | relative_url }}) personnel, Flight 4 was launched from Alamogordo on 4 June 1947 as part of the programme's early field tests. The balloon train was far larger and more complex than an ordinary weather balloon, consisting of multiple balloons, radar-reflector components and experimental equipment designed to study long-range acoustic detection. The key claim is that the flight travelled generally north-eastward across central New Mexico. Later reconstructions by Project Mogul engineer Charles Moore suggested that the balloon train likely moved towards the Corona region before disappearing from tracking. That route matters because Corona lies close to the ranch where W.W. “Mac” Brazel later found the scattered debris that triggered the Roswell incident.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cdn.centerforinquiry.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/1995/07/22165104/p17.pdf?_ga=2.167806568.87202448.1674674099-1555719303.1674674099" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdn.centerforinquiry.org">[Center for Inquiry]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdn.centerforinquiry.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Flight 4 lifted off from Alamogordo, it probably ascended while traveling northeast (toward Arabela), then turned...Read more...</span></span></span>

Sceptical researchers who accept the Mogul explanation often point to this geographical overlap. The argument is not that investigators can trace every mile of the flight with certainty, but that Flight 4 is the only known Mogul launch that fits the timing, location and recovery gap well enough to serve as a plausible source of the debris.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/2017/12/the-roswell-incident-at-70-facts-not-myths/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[skepticalinquirer.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Skeptical Inquirer The Roswell Incident at 70: Facts, Not Myths4</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The research team launched NYU Flight #4 on June 4, 1947, from Alamogordo Army Air Field and tracked it flying east-northeast toward Coro...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 4, 1947</span></span></span>

## Why the Unrecovered Flight Matters

The Roswell mystery depends heavily on a missing object. If the military had launched a balloon train, tracked it to landing and recovered it, there would be little reason to connect it with Brazel's discovery. Flight 4 became important precisely because it appears to have vanished from the project's normal recovery process.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muller.lbl.gov/teaching/physics10/Roswell/RoswellIncident.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muller.lbl.gov">[Muller Lab]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muller.lbl.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Muller LabROSWELL INCIDENT REPORT... The Air Force's 1994 and 1995 studies concluded that material found near Roswell was most likely associated with an unrecovered Mogul flight, specifically the June 4 launch. Researchers examining project records argued that the disappearance of Flight 4 created a realistic opportunity for debris to remain on the ground long enough to be discovered later by a rancher unfamiliar with classified balloon equipment. ## Why the Documentation Became Controversial

Flight 4 would not be controversial if the paperwork were clear. The problem is that surviving records from the programme are incomplete, and different researchers have interpreted them in different ways.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/AFD-101201-038.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dafhistory.af.mil">[DAF History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dafhistory.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DAF HistoryThe Roswell Report... Project MOGUL, the top-priority classified project of balloon-borne experiments, which provides the expl...</span></span></span>

One side of the debate relies heavily on project notes, launch records and recollections from Mogul participants. These sources support the view that Flight 4 was launched on 4 June and later lost. The Air Force's Roswell reports treated that conclusion as the most likely reconstruction of events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/AFD-101201-038.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dafhistory.af.mil">[af.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dafhistory.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DAF HistoryThe Roswell Report... Project MOGUL, the top-priority classified project of balloon-borne experiments, which provides the expl...</span></span></span>

Critics focus on apparent gaps and inconsistencies. Some researchers have argued that records indicate the planned Flight 4 launch was cancelled because of weather conditions and therefore could not have produced debris.
