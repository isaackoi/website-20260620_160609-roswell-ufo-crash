## Introduction

One of the strongest points in Sheridan [Cavitt]({{ 'cavitt/' | relative_url }})’s ordinary-material account of the Roswell debris is not simply that he described commonplace objects, but that those objects closely resemble the construction materials used in 1940s radar targets carried beneath military [balloon trains]({{ 'balloon-train/' | relative_url }}). Cavitt recalled reflective foil-like material, thin bamboo-like sticks, and a small instrument component rather than heavy metal, engines, or advanced machinery. Those details match the known design of radar reflectors used on balloon projects far more closely than they resemble the wreckage of a sophisticated aircraft or spacecraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/ufo/report_af_roswell.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nsa.gov">[National Security Agency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nsa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">balsa wood sticks, affixed with tape, glue, and twine.Read more</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Security Agencyreport of air force research regarding theMarch 24, 2018 — 21 Jul 1994 — These targets were made up of aluminum &quot;...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 24, 2018</span></span></span>

## Foil-Backed Paper and Light Supports

Cavitt's affidavit and later interviews described debris consisting of reflective material resembling aluminium foil together with thin bamboo-like sticks. Those are not random features. The U.S. [Air Force]({{ 'air-force/' | relative_url }})'s Roswell investigation identified early radar targets used in balloon programmes as being constructed from aluminium foil or foil-backed paper attached to lightweight balsa-wood members. The same reports note the use of reinforcing tape, twine, glue, and other light materials designed to keep the reflector airborne while adding minimal weight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/ufo/report_af_roswell.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nsa.gov">[nsa.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nsa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">balsa wood sticks, affixed with tape, glue, and twine.Read more</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Security Agencyreport of air force research regarding theMarch 24, 2018 — 21 Jul 1994 — These targets were made up of aluminum &quot;...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 24, 2018</span></span></span>

This matters because radar reflectors were engineered to be as light as possible. A large metallic structure would have been impractical beneath high-altitude balloons. Instead, designers used:

* Foil or foil-backed paper to create radar-reflective surfaces.
* Lightweight balsa-wood supports. [[media]({{ 'media/' | relative_url }}). defense.gov](#endnote-1 "Snippet: Department of WarU.S. Air Force: 'The Roswell Report: Case Closed'taped to a balsa wood frame, several ML-307B/AP radar targets were used")
* Tape and glue to join sections.
* String, twine, and small fittings to suspend the assembly.
* Compact meteorological instruments attached to the balloon train.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/ufo/report_af_roswell.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nsa.gov">[National Security Agency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nsa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">balsa wood sticks, affixed with tape, glue, and twine.Read more</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Security Agencyreport of air force research regarding theMarch 24, 2018 — 21 Jul 1994 — These targets were made up of aluminum &quot;...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 24, 2018</span></span></span>

A witness encountering the wreckage after weather exposure might reasonably describe the wooden members as bamboo-like sticks. Cavitt's wording therefore aligns with the appearance of the materials rather than with their exact manufacturing specifications.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ciphermysteries.com/2026/01/15/the-roswell-capsule-was-it-tex-settles-flying-coffin" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ciphermysteries.com">[Cipher Mysteries]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ciphermysteries.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the roswell capsule was it tex settles flying coffin</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cipher MysteriesThe Roswell capsule: was it Tex Settle&#x27;s Flying Coffin?15 Jan 2026 — He stated unequivocally, however, that the material...</span></span></span>

## Box-Kite-Style Reflector Construction

The physical design of the radar targets is especially relevant. Air Force documentation describes the reflectors as multi-faced structures assembled from foil-covered surfaces supported by balsa beams and connected with tape, twine, eyelets, and glue. The reports compare their construction to a box kite.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/ufo/report_af_roswell.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nsa.gov">[National Security Agency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nsa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">balsa wood sticks, affixed with tape, glue, and twine.Read more</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Security Agencyreport of air force research regarding theMarch 24, 2018 — 21 Jul 1994 — These targets were made up of aluminum &quot;...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 24, 2018</span></span></span>

* Reflective foil surfaces.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://ciphermysteries.com/2026/01/15/the-roswell-capsule-was-it-tex-settles-flying-coffin" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ciphermysteries.com">[ciphermysteries.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ciphermysteries.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the roswell capsule was it tex settles flying coffin</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cipher MysteriesThe Roswell capsule: was it Tex Settle&#x27;s Flying Coffin?15 Jan 2026 — He stated unequivocally, however, that the material...</span></span></span> [* Thin wooden sticks.](#endnote-14 "Snippet:<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.ufosandusos.com/Tab%202_REV.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufosandusos.com">[USAF 1995 report]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufosandusos.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tab 2 REV</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">USAF 1995 report].Read more...</span></span></span>. Read more")
* Adhesive tape.
* String and connecting hardware.
* Pieces of balloon material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/ufo/report_af_roswell.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nsa.gov">[National Security Agency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nsa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">balsa wood sticks, affixed with tape, glue, and twine.Read more</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Security Agencyreport of air force research regarding theMarch 24, 2018 — 21 Jul 1994 — These targets were made up of aluminum &quot;...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 24, 2018</span></span></span>

That breakdown pattern helps explain why multiple contemporary descriptions of the Roswell debris mention combinations of foil, paper, rubber, tape, and sticks rather than large structural components. Contemporary accounts from 1947 similarly referred to tinfoil, paper, tape, [rubber strips]({{ 'rubber-strips/' | relative_url }}), and sticks gathered from the ranch.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roswell_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Roswell incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell incident</span></span></span>
